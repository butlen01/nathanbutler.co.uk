Growth

Redesigning pay to redesign growth

How a cost-neutral pay-model redesign turned two competing teams into one aligned engine — and became the incentive language for the whole division.

4× divisional revenue growth, seven years

Situation

Revenue growth had stalled at the level individual effort could sustain. The incentive structure in place rewarded activity — a team winning an instruction was paid regardless of what happened next — not the handoff between winning work and actually delivering it. Two teams that needed to cooperate had no formal reason to.

Growth depended on a handful of strong performers who managed to bridge that gap personally, rather than on a system that made cooperation the default.

Approach

  • Redesigned a long-standing pay framework into a "Win:Sell" model — one team winning instructions, one team selling them, on jointly owned targets, so both sides were paid on the same outcome.
  • Made the change cost-neutral, redirecting existing incentive spend rather than adding to it — no one was worse off on day one, which removed the biggest source of resistance to a pay-model change.
  • Rolled the model out as the common incentive language across local, regional and national teams, so growth stopped depending on any one office's informal working relationships.
  • Kept the model under review rather than treating the redesign as a one-off fix — pricing, targets and structure were retested on a regular cadence as the market moved.

Result

Revenue grew more than fourfold over seven years — from c.£1.9m to c.£8.2m — sustained through a cyclical market, not a rising one. The model still runs, largely unchanged, years after it was introduced.
