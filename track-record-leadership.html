01 BDM Structure
£1.1bn GDV pipeline, contracts secured

A BDM Structure Built to Scale, Local to National

Situation

BDMs were aligned purely by geography — sensible on paper, but it meant national and regional housebuilders could hear different pitches, fees and service levels from different parts of the same business, with almost no coordination with the agency network that would ultimately sell the stock. Success came down to individual entrepreneurialism, not a strategy.

Approach
  • Rolled out a Win:Sell model with the agency network — my team won the instruction, their branches sold it off daily footfall they already had, and the more we won, the more they sold, the more they wanted to help us win again
  • Set clear lanes for who approached whom: PLC developers coordinated centrally between Regional Sales Directors, SMEs handled directly by BDMs, national relationships kept at my level — the same standard of service wherever the account sat
  • Built a land team to bring developers opportunities they didn't already have, rather than pitch cold for stock they controlled — turning the approach from asking a favour into trading one
Result

A pipeline built on structure and reciprocity, not luck — c.£1.1bn of GDV under contract, sourced through relationships designed to repeat.

02 Growth
4× divisional revenue growth, seven years

Redesigning Pay to Redesign Growth

Situation

Variable pay was tied to the revenue running through an account, not to the outcome of it. Whoever won a new mandate had every incentive to hold onto it personally — genuinely believing they could run it better — even when it should have moved on to whoever could actually deliver it.

Approach
  • Rebuilt variable pay around the milestones of a mandate instead of its ongoing revenue: 25% on signature and due diligence, 25% on the first invoice clearing, the balance on the home actually selling
  • Weighted the split deliberately 50/50 — enough on the controllable, immediate actions to remove the incentive to hoard a mandate, but enough held back on the final sale that winning a bad instruction was never rewarded as a win
  • Stress-tested the new structure financially across dozens of deal scenarios before rollout, so the business could fund it sustainably whatever the mix of deal size and timing
Result

Revenue grew more than fourfold over seven years — c.£1.9m to c.£8.2m — sustained through a cyclical market, not a rising one.

03 Margin Discipline
23% ROS return on sale, sustained

Holding Margin While the Market Squeezed It

Situation

UK new-build completions fell a cumulative 10.2% over three years, with quarterly output contractions reported by the ONS throughout. Selling more is the easy lever for return on sale — past a threshold, incremental revenue is largely profit — but that lever barely works when the market itself is shrinking.

Approach
  • Stripped the cost of winning business itself — developers now paid for site boards and hoarding the business had previously given away free, run on the principle of treating the purse as if it were my own, not cutting to a number
  • Pushed productivity per head hard enough to take an unfair share of a shrinking market — more mandates won meant more to sell, even as the market itself contracted
  • Held firm on fee negotiations rather than discounting to win work, earning the price through demonstrated pricing and go-to-market advice, or land opportunities brought directly to the developer
Result

A sustained 23% return on sale, while industry-average returns were being squeezed from 15% down to 10% in the same market.

04 Turnaround
Loss → Profit a unit earmarked for closure

Rescuing a Business Earmarked for Closure

Situation

A Scottish business unit was loss-making and earmarked for closure — not for lack of revenue, but because the cost carried against it had grown disproportionate. Every contract needed the same question asked of it: genuinely unsellable, or a pricing and marketing problem still worth fixing?

Approach
  • Audited every contract for profitability rather than treating the region as one problem — a marketing fix, a renegotiation with the developer on price, or an exit, depending on what was actually broken
  • Raised the rate card despite thin volume — holding fees guaranteed the business would close, raising them only risked it, and that was the only bet worth taking
  • Rebuilt the cost base site by site: some stayed staffed on-site, some moved to selling from local offices, and on others the developer took on the staffing cost while the business kept management control
  • Restructured a management layer that was making decisions without contributing to them — mapped the roles the business actually needed and let go only those causing the culture of blame, not headcount for its own sake
Result

The unit became one of the division's stronger performers — anchored by landmark wins like The Victoria in Glasgow — and the recovery earned promotion to Divisional Director.

05 Revenue Diversification
Cost → Profit two cost centres turned self-funding

Turning Cost Centres Into Profit Centres

Situation

Marketing and market research were treated as pure cost centres — necessary spend with no expectation of return — while financial services relied entirely on third-party providers, so that income sat outside the business altogether.

Approach
  • Changed the report format market research produced and started selling it — up to £15k a time, developer by developer, with training rolled out to every BDM to support the pitch
  • Put a 10% levy on every piece of marketing material produced on a developer's behalf — turning a cost too small to bill for individually into real upside charged per developer
  • Engaged financial-services stakeholders directly to build a bespoke arrangement, accruing a share of the incremental income it created — adding an average £560 per sale to the P&L at close to zero marginal cost
Result

Marketing and market research became self-funding income streams, protecting both from budget cuts, while four of five divisions ranked in the group's top four of thirty-six for cross-sell.

06 Trusted Relationships
c.2,500 units c.£12.5m personal contribution, 35% new business overall

Trusted Enough to Stay in the Room

Situation

The biggest wins didn't come from a single big pitch — they came from staying in relationships long after most people would have moved on, and from being trusted with national accounts run through office networks and managers entirely outside the formal reporting line.

Approach
  • Pursued a landmark 500-unit Liverpool conversion for seven years, one of the largest single instructions in the account's history, while holding accounts like Sanctuary, Thriving (PfP), CBRE and Octopus Real Estate through consultative advice first, then delivery
  • Built a genuinely structural understanding of both businesses — not just what the client wanted, but how their organisation worked and where it interlocked with mine — and trained the team to pace value deliberately rather than front-load it
  • Kept the relationship alive on both sides, engaging the stakeholders inside my own business who controlled the capacity and flexibility a long account actually needs, not just the client-facing side of it
Result

c.2,500 units won personally — c.£12.5m of contribution — with new-business wins up 35% year on year overall, built on relationships that outlasted any single deal.

07 Governance
£1.4m year-on-year rise in new-business wins, Q1

An Extra 1%, Every Day

Situation

There was no control over how data was recorded, or how it was used to move the business forward — no way to know how many potential buyers visited a site over a weekend, and no verified record of BDM conversations beyond trusting what people said they'd done.

Approach
  • Owned the CRM build end to end — procurement, contract negotiation, design, training and ongoing use — then made its data the only way anyone got credit for their work, which settled the adoption problem on its own
  • Designed the management information and governance that ran off that data — activity-point targets and a live view of the business that turned coaching from instinct into evidence
  • Refreshed the marketing collateral in parallel — professional but with real warmth, standardised but adaptable — so the same CRM data could show exactly where an individual's results tracked the plan and where they didn't
Result

A £1.4m year-on-year rise in new-business wins in the first full quarter, with a single source of truth running the business in real time — from site footfall to BD activity — instead of manual effort and trust.
