Track Record & Leadership

The number, and how it was built.

Thirteen commercial, operational and people systems, each rebuilt from the ground up inside a business of five divisions, c.80 people and a 250-office delivery network. Every one begins with the result, then shows the structure, the decisions and the people that produced it. The pattern matters more than any single figure. These are repeatable methods that outlasted my involvement rather than outcomes that depended on it, and none of them are specific to property.

The Commercial Model

“Win the client mandate through consultative B2B advice on go-to-market and pricing, then own the B2C execution that delivers it, and be measured on both.” That is the operating model behind every result on this page. It applies to any business that has to win a client, deliver what it sold, and be judged on how the end customer experienced it. The sector changes. The mechanics do not.

Thirteen Proof Points

Results, and the systems behind them.

Growth & Structure

Four different engines for winning new business, or rescuing one that had stopped.

01 Business Development
£1.1bn GDV pipeline, contracts secured

Structural Alignment: A Business Development Engine Built to Scale

Thirty business development managers, thirty-eight agency regions outside my control, and no reason for the two to help each other.

Aligning them, without ever forcing a rigid one-to-one grid, built a £1.1bn pipeline that kept producing after I stopped holding it together.
02 Incentive Design
4× divisional revenue growth, seven years

Redesigning Pay to Redesign Growth

The pay model rewarded holding onto an account, so people held on, sincerely believing they could run it better.

Redesigning what got paid, and when, cost the business nothing and grew revenue fourfold.
03 Formal Procurement
Scored on Paper won on evaluation rather than access

Winning the Contract Before the Relationship Can Help You

A twenty-year relationship earns you an invitation. Then you are scored on paper by evaluators who have never met you.

Writing to the criteria instead of the story is how the paper got stronger every single cycle.
04 Turnaround
Loss → Profit a unit earmarked for closure

Rescuing a Business Earmarked for Closure

A standalone Scottish unit, loss-making and marked for closure, wasn't short of revenue. It was carrying cost that had grown disproportionate to it.

Raising fees when volume was already thin looked like the wrong call. It was the only one that saved the unit.
Commercial Discipline

Protecting margin, creating new revenue, and the reporting that made every number trustworthy.

05 Margin Discipline
23% ROS return on sale, sustained

Holding Margin While the Market Squeezed It

New-build completions fell 10.2% in three years, and industry-average returns fell from 15% to 10% alongside them.

Four levers, pulled at once, none of them about selling harder, held mine at 23% the whole way through.
06 Revenue Diversification
+£560 average added per sale, at close to zero marginal cost

Bringing New-Homes Business Back to an In-House Function

The group's own financial services arm existed the whole time. The new-homes division sent its business to outside providers instead.

Winning the trust to bring it in-house added £560 a sale at close to zero marginal cost.
07 Cost → Profit
£15k per research report, developer by developer

Turning Research and Marketing Into Profit Centres

Two cost centres nobody had ever asked to earn their keep, exactly when every line of spend was under scrutiny.

Both became income lines, with reports alone selling for up to £15,000 a time.
08 CRM & Control
£1.4m year-on-year rise in new-business wins, Q1

An Extra 1%, Every Day

Business-development contacts and mandates lived in nothing more than somebody's own mobile phone, theirs to keep or delete.

Replacing that with a single source of truth added £1.4m in new-business wins in a single quarter.
09 Board Discipline
5 Divisions one P&L, one forecast, one accountable owner

Owning the Number in Front of the Board

Five divisions, c.80 people and nine direct reports, one number to defend in front of the group, through a market that contracted for three straight years.

Building a budget nobody could pick apart is what bought the freedom to run it at pace.
People & Client Outcomes

What happened once the account was won, and who was in the room to keep winning it.

10 Affordable Housing
760 Homes delivered in the final year

Bringing a National Affordable Housing Operation Under Divisional Leadership

A specialist national team, a different buyer, a different regulator, absorbed into divisional leadership without flattening what made it work.

760 homes delivered in the final year, plus a second revenue line nobody had been tapping.
11 Trusted Relationships
c.4,500 units c.£22.5m won personally

Trusted Enough to Stay in the Room

Seven years pursuing one building, handing over strategic advice for free with no signed instruction. Competitors circled and dropped away.

The instruction was worth the wait.
12 Customer Outcomes
Board-Reported client-scored, on the largest account

The Customer Was the Product

On the largest account in the group, my delivery was measured by the client's own buyers and reported to the client's own board.

That changes the job permanently, and it shows up ten years later as not a single formal complaint.
13 Building the Bench
5 of 6 senior roles filled from within

Grow What You Can, Buy What You Can't

Regional leadership in this business is specialised, dispersed and hard to hire at level.

Five of six senior roles came up through a ladder I built. The one I bought instead still changed the unit economics.
