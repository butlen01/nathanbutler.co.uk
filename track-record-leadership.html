Track Record & Leadership

The number, and how it was built.

Fourteen commercial, operational and people systems, each rebuilt from the ground up inside a business of five divisions, c.80 people and a 250-office delivery network. Every one begins with the result, then shows the structure, the decisions and the people that produced it. The pattern matters more than any single figure. These are repeatable methods that outlasted my involvement rather than outcomes that depended on it, and none of them are specific to property.

×4 divisional revenue 23% ROS sustained £1.1bn GDV pipeline 100% account retention c.80 people, 250 offices
The Commercial Model

“Win the client mandate through consultative B2B advice on go-to-market and pricing, then own the B2C execution that delivers it, and be measured on both.” That is the operating model behind every result on this page. It applies to any business that has to win a client, deliver what it sold, and be judged on how the end customer experienced it. The sector changes. The mechanics do not.

Growth

Three different engines for winning new business: structure, incentives and formal tender.

01 BDM Structure
£1.1bn GDV pipeline, contracts secured

A BDM Structure Built to Scale, Local to National

Situation

Business development managers were aligned purely by geography. Sensible on paper, but in practice national and regional housebuilders heard different pitches, fees and service levels from different parts of the same business, with almost no coordination with the agency network that would ultimately sell the stock. Growth depended on individual entrepreneurialism rather than a structure, which meant it could not be forecast, protected or scaled.

Approach
  • Rolled out a Win:Sell model with the agency network: my team won the instruction, their branches sold it off daily footfall they already had, and the more my team won, the more they sold, the more they wanted to help win again
  • Set clear lanes for who approached whom: PLC developers coordinated centrally between Regional Sales Directors, SMEs handled directly by BDMs, national relationships kept at my level, the same standard of service wherever the account sat
  • Built a land team to bring developers opportunities they didn't already have, rather than pitch cold for stock they controlled, turning the approach from asking a favour into trading one
Result

A pipeline built on structure and reciprocity rather than luck: c.£1.1bn of GDV under contract, sourced through relationships designed to repeat. It kept producing after I stopped holding it together personally, which is the real test of whether it was ever a system at all.

02 Growth
4× divisional revenue growth, seven years

Redesigning Pay to Redesign Growth

Situation

Variable pay was tied to the revenue running through an account, not to the outcome of it. Whoever won a new mandate had every incentive to hold onto it personally, genuinely believing they could run it better, even when it should have moved on to whoever could actually deliver it.

Approach
  • Rebuilt variable pay around the milestones of a mandate instead of its ongoing revenue: 25% on signature and due diligence, 25% on the first invoice clearing, the balance on the home actually selling
  • Weighted the split deliberately 50/50, enough on the controllable, immediate actions to remove the incentive to hoard a mandate, but enough held back on the final sale that winning a bad instruction was never rewarded as a win
  • Stress-tested the new structure financially across dozens of deal scenarios before rollout, so the business could fund it sustainably whatever the mix of deal size and timing
Result

Revenue grew more than fourfold over seven years, from c.£1.9m to c.£8.2m, and it held through a cyclical market rather than riding a rising one. The behaviour changed before the revenue did, because people were finally paid for the outcome the business needed, not the one that was easiest to bank.

03 Formal Procurement
Formal Tender public and institutional procurement, won on evaluation rather than access

Winning the Contract Before the Relationship Can Help You

Situation

A significant share of the business I was responsible for could not be won through relationships. Housing associations, local authorities, institutional funds and government-backed programmes buy through formal procurement, scored on paper by evaluators who may never have met me and are not permitted to be influenced by the fact that they might have.

Approach
  • Led the business's formal bid submissions personally, including government tenders, owning the commercial position, pricing model and quality response rather than delegating the number
  • Wrote to the published evaluation weightings rather than to the story, and made a clear qualify-or-decline call before anyone wrote a word
  • Made operational depth the differentiator: the detail built up on a live account over years couldn't be matched by a competitor inside a six-week bid window
Result

Substantial contract wins with housing associations, private developers, local authorities and institutional funds, and the largest account in the group retained in full through every tender cycle of the relationship.

Protecting the Margin

Holding or creating margin when the market pushed the other way.

04 Margin Discipline
23% ROS return on sale, sustained

Holding Margin While the Market Squeezed It

Situation

UK new-build completions fell a cumulative 10.2% over three years, with the ONS reporting quarterly output contractions throughout. In a rising market the easy route to margin is to sell more, because past a certain threshold incremental revenue is largely profit. That lever barely works when the market is contracting, so the margin had to come from how the business was run, not from volume it was never going to get.

Approach
  • Stripped the cost of winning business itself: developers now paid for site boards and hoarding the business had previously given away free, run on the principle of treating the purse as if it were my own, not cutting to a number
  • Pushed productivity per head hard enough to take an unfair share of a shrinking market: more mandates won meant more to sell, even as the market itself contracted
  • Held firm on fee negotiations rather than discounting to win work, earning the price through demonstrated pricing and go-to-market advice, or land opportunities brought directly to the developer
Result

A sustained 23% return on sale, held while industry-average returns were squeezed from 15% to 10% in the same market. The gap widened rather than narrowed over the period, and none of it came from selling more into a market that was not there to sell into.

05 Turnaround
Loss → Profit a unit earmarked for closure

Rescuing a Business Earmarked for Closure

Situation

A Scottish business unit was loss-making and earmarked for closure, not for lack of revenue but because the cost carried against that revenue had grown out of proportion to it. It also carried a management layer that had grown heavy, making decisions without contributing to the result. Fixing it meant asking the same honest question of every contract: genuinely unsellable, or a pricing and marketing problem still worth solving?

Approach
  • Audited every contract for profitability rather than treating the region as one problem: a marketing fix, a renegotiation with the developer on price, or an exit, depending on what was actually broken
  • Raised the rate card despite thin volume: holding fees guaranteed the business would close, raising them only risked it, and that was the only bet worth taking
  • Rebuilt the cost base site by site: some stayed staffed on-site, some moved to selling from local offices, and on others the developer took on the staffing cost while the business kept management control
  • Restructured a management layer that was making decisions without contributing to them, running proper consultation and matching people into the structure the business genuinely needed, not headcount removed to hit a number
Result

The unit became one of the division's stronger performers, carried by a cost base that finally matched its revenue and a pipeline built on anchor instructions rather than hope. Landmark wins such as The Victoria in Glasgow proved the recovery was real, and it earned promotion to Divisional Director.

06 Revenue Diversification
Cost → Profit two cost centres turned self-funding

Turning Cost Centres Into Profit Centres

Situation

Marketing and market research were treated as pure cost centres: necessary spend with no expectation of a return. Financial services followed the same pattern from a different angle, relying on third-party providers so the income sat outside the business entirely. Three lines that only ever took money out, in a period when every cost was under scrutiny.

Approach
  • Changed the report format market research produced and started selling it, up to £15k a time, developer by developer, with training rolled out to every BDM to support the pitch
  • Put a modest levy on every piece of marketing material produced on a developer's behalf, turning a cost too small to bill for individually into real upside charged per developer
  • Engaged financial-services stakeholders directly to build a bespoke arrangement that shared the incremental income it created, adding an average £560 per sale to the P&L at close to zero marginal cost
Result

Marketing and market research became self-funding income streams, which protected both from the next round of budget cuts, and four of five divisions ranked in the group's top four of thirty-six for cross-sell. Financial services alone added an average of £560 per sale at close to zero marginal cost, one of the cleanest routes to a higher return on sale there is.

07 Revenue Diversification
+£560 average added per sale, at close to zero marginal cost

Bringing an In-House Function Back In-House

Situation

Countrywide Financial Services, the group's own financial services arm, existed the whole time, but the new-homes division routed almost all of its mortgage and protection business to outside providers instead, a service model that had never been shaped around new-build, off-plan buyers.

Approach
  • Made the case in commercial terms Countrywide Financial Services' own leadership would recognise: not a favour, but a referral stream that had been leaving the group for years, available to bring in-house if the service model changed to fit it
  • Built a bespoke service model around new-homes buyers specifically and integrated it directly into the sales process, raised at the point a buyer was already committing rather than chased afterwards as a referral
  • Structured the arrangement so the division shared in the income the change created, an internal revenue share rather than a one-off favour, which is what made it durable
Result

£560 average added per sale at close to zero marginal cost, on income that had been leaving the group entirely, plus mortgage and protection advice finally built around the rhythm of a new-build purchase rather than a standard high street process.

08 Cost → Profit
Top 4 of 36 divisions ranked for cross-sell, four out of five

Turning Research and Marketing Into Profit Centres

Situation

Market research and marketing were run as pure overhead: necessary spend with no expectation of a return, and exactly the kind of cost line that gets cut first when a budget tightens.

Approach
  • Changed the format the research team produced, from an internal document into something a developer would actually pay for, and started selling it directly, up to £15k a time, with training rolled out to every BDM to support the pitch
  • Put a modest levy on every piece of marketing material produced on a developer's behalf, turning a cost too small to bill for individually into real, per-developer upside
  • Made the case twice over, to the teams themselves and to the wider business, that charging for what had always been free wouldn't damage relationships built over years
Result

Two cost centres turned into income lines, which protected both from the next budget round rather than making them a target for it. Four of five divisions ranked in the group's top four of thirty-six for cross-sell.

Governance and Control

The reporting, forecasting and accountability that make a P&L trustworthy.

09 Governance
£1.4m year-on-year rise in new-business wins, Q1

An Extra 1%, Every Day

Situation

There was no control over how data was recorded, or how it was used to move the business forward. No reliable way to know how many potential buyers had visited a site over a weekend, and no verified record of business development activity beyond trusting what people said they had done. The business ran on trust where it should have run on evidence.

Approach
  • Owned the CRM build end to end, procurement, contract negotiation, design, training and ongoing use, then made its data the only way anyone got credit for their work, which settled the adoption problem on its own
  • Designed the management information and governance that ran off that data: activity-point targets and a live view of the business that turned coaching from instinct into evidence
  • Refreshed the marketing collateral in parallel, professional but with real warmth, standardised but adaptable, so the same CRM data could show exactly where an individual's results tracked the plan and where they didn't
Result

A £1.4m year-on-year rise in new-business wins in the first full quarter, with a single source of truth running the business in real time, from site footfall to business development activity. The same cadence still drives coaching today, which is what turns a system into a habit rather than a one-off intervention.

10 Board Discipline
5 Divisions one P&L, one forecast, one accountable owner

Owning the Number in Front of the Board

Situation

Everything else on this page sits underneath one thing: I owned the budget. Five divisions, a national affordable housing operation, c.80 people and nine direct reports, with the annual budget, the rolling forecast and the long-term growth plan all built by me and defended upward to the group.

Approach
  • Built the budget from the bottom up, from pipeline, conversion rates and an actual cost base, so the number was defensible line by line before it was ever committed
  • Ran a monthly cadence of forecast, variance and action, with the variance explained before anyone had to ask, and held nine direct reports to their own numbers on the same cadence
  • Worked the matrix deliberately: made the case to finance, legal, marketing, HR and operations in the terms each function was measured on, and kept the three-year growth plan separate from the twelve-month budget
Result

Consecutive years of revenue and volume growth, delivered against budget and forecast rather than around them, through a market that contracted for three of those years. A group centre that trusts a forecast stops managing the detail, which is what let five dispersed divisions run at genuine pace.

11 Affordable Housing
760 Homes delivered in the final year

Bringing a National Affordable Housing Operation Under Divisional Leadership

Situation

Affordable housing ran as a separate national team, selling shared ownership and affordable tenures to a different buyer profile, on a different funding model, against a regulatory framework that had almost nothing in common with open market new build. Bringing it under divisional leadership meant absorbing a specialist operation into a general one without flattening the specialism that made it work.

Approach

A small team, under ten people, already delivering but at levels that undersold what a specialist unit that size could produce. The job was growth and integration together: raise output without flattening the different buyer, different regulator and different route to market that made the team distinct in the first place.

Grew affordable housing from 10% to 35% of the division's revenue, a deliberate ceiling rather than a natural one: other divisions let the same income stream drift toward 70% of their own number in some instances, and grew comfortable enough on it to win proportionately less new business elsewhere. The team covered roughly half the country and still delivered close to 45% of the group's entire national affordable housing revenue, work worth something like 60–65% of everything every other division delivered across all their revenue streams combined.

Result

760 units delivered in the final year, added into the divisional P&L, with a client base of registered providers, local authorities and institutional funds that tenders through formal procurement and buys on delivery evidence rather than relationship. It diversified the division's revenue away from open market cyclicality at exactly the point the open market was contracting.

12 Trusted Relationships
c.4,500 units c.£22.5m won personally, 35% new business overall

Trusted Enough to Stay in the Room

Situation

The biggest wins did not come from a single big pitch. They came from staying in relationships long after most people would have moved on, and from being trusted with national accounts that ran through office networks and managers entirely outside my formal reporting line. Retention on that scale is not a relationship skill, it is a commercial one.

Approach
  • Pursued the Tobacco Warehouse, a landmark 500-unit Liverpool conversion, for seven years, one of the largest single instructions in the account's history, while holding accounts like Sanctuary, Thriving (PfP), CBRE and Octopus Real Estate through consultative advice first, then delivery
  • Built a genuinely structural understanding of both businesses, not just what the client wanted, but how their organisation worked and where it interlocked with mine, and trained the team to pace value deliberately rather than front-load it
  • Kept the relationship alive on both sides, engaging the stakeholders inside my own business who controlled the capacity and flexibility a long account actually needs, not just the client-facing side of it
Result

c.4,500 units won personally across seven years, worth c.£22.5m in fee value, with around 40% contracted and still to release against future build programmes. New-business wins rose 35% year on year and every national account was retained. The wins outlasted the deals that started them, which is the point: relationships built to repeat, not to peak in the first meeting.

13 Customer Outcomes
Board-Reported client-scored customer satisfaction, the measure I was held to on the largest account

The Customer Was the Product

Situation

On the largest account in the group, I was not primarily measured on sales. Sanctuary surveyed its own buyers and reported those scores directly to its own board, so a weak month on one site became a line in a report the client's board read every quarter.

Approach
  • Trained site teams to the standard a client's board expected rather than the level the contract required, and designed buyer journeys from nothing on council-owned developments
  • Built compliance around the customer, not just the client: affordability testing, income caps and audit trails proven before a regulator ever asked, on a Homes England-funded shared ownership portfolio
  • Held delivery standards across an agency network of c.250 offices outside my reporting line by setting the model and KPIs those networks would own, and visited every site personally
Result

Every national account retained. A 150-flat scheme sold out entirely off-plan and a 500-unit tower sold 45% off-plan with the remainder inside twelve months, the cleanest evidence that pricing and specification were right for the buyer.

People and Capability

How the leadership team itself was built.

14 Building the Bench
5 of 6 senior roles filled from within, the sixth from the client side

Nobody Was Recruited Into the Leadership Team

Situation

Regional leadership in this business is specialised, dispersed and hard to hire at level. The market for somebody who understands a developer client, a 250-office agency network and a P&L at the same time is thin, expensive and slow, and every senior vacancy filled externally costs months of momentum. The alternative is to build the ladder so the next person is already most of the way up it before the vacancy exists.

Approach
  • Built a five-rung ladder inside one discipline, business development manager through to Senior Residential Development Director, so somebody was always one step below every role that mattered and already doing a version of it
  • Gave people on progression plans oversight of live projects with structured feedback rather than shadowing, and had those closest to promotion deliver the training and onboarding themselves
  • Buddied directors to one another so mentoring ran between peers rather than through me, kept separate from coaching, which I did myself because their performance was my number
  • Bought the one capability a sales business cannot grow internally: a Land Director with major acquisition and finance expertise, who had been my client for ten years
Result

Five of six senior roles filled from inside, including two Residential Development Directors who started as business development managers. The Land Director changed the unit economics rather than the headcount: materially larger sites transacted for broadly the same effort, and a bigger site carries more downstream units and more cross-sold revenue behind it. And several people I developed left, reached Head of Sales and Sales Director level with developers, and then put business my way.
