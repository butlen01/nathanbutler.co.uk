01 BDM Structure
£1.1bn GDV pipeline, contracts secured

A BDM Structure Built to Scale, Local to National

Situation

BDMs were aligned purely by geography — sensible on paper, but it meant national and regional housebuilders could hear different pitches, fees and service levels from different parts of the same business, with almost no coordination with the agency network that would ultimately sell the stock. Success came down to individual entrepreneurialism, not a strategy.

Approach
  • Rolled out a Win:Sell model with the agency network — my team won the instruction, their branches sold it off daily footfall they already had, and the more we won, the more they sold, the more they wanted to help us win again
  • Set clear lanes for who approached whom: PLC developers coordinated centrally between Regional Sales Directors, SMEs handled directly by BDMs, national relationships kept at my level — the same standard of service wherever the account sat
  • Built a land team to bring developers opportunities they didn't already have, rather than pitch cold for stock they controlled — turning the approach from asking a favour into trading one
Result

A pipeline built on structure and reciprocity, not luck — c.£1.1bn of GDV under contract, sourced through relationships designed to repeat.

02 Growth
4× divisional revenue growth, seven years

Redesigning pay to redesign growth

Situation

Revenue growth had stalled at the level individual effort could sustain — the incentive structure rewarded activity, not the handoff between winning work and delivering it, so growth depended on a handful of strong performers rather than the system underneath them.

Approach
  • Redesigned a long-standing pay framework into a "Win:Sell" model — one team winning instructions, one team selling them, on jointly owned targets
  • Made the change cost-neutral, redirecting existing incentive spend rather than adding to it, so no one was worse off on day one
  • Rolled the model out as the common incentive language across local, regional and national teams, so growth stopped depending on any one office
Result

Revenue grew more than fourfold over seven years — c.£1.9m to c.£8.2m — sustained through a cyclical market, not a rising one.

03 TBC
23% ROS return on sale, sustained

Holding margin while the market squeezed it

Situation

Return on sale was under constant pressure — industry averages were being squeezed from 15% down to 10% — and the easy costs had already been cut, so the next gain had to come from how the business was run, not further cost-cutting.

Approach
  • Took direct control of the cost base, closing out loss-making lines rather than trimming around them
  • Brought group services in-house where it removed duplicated cost and gave the division direct control of its own margin
  • Built the reporting and pipeline discipline to see margin erosion early, not after the numbers had already moved
Result

A sustained 23% return on sale, while industry-average returns were being squeezed from 15% down to 10% in the same market.

04 Turnaround
Loss → Profit a unit earmarked for closure

Rescuing a business earmarked for closure

Situation

A Scottish business unit was six figures loss-making and earmarked for closure, its contract base built on unprofitable referral arrangements it couldn't sustain — and the team had stopped believing it could be turned around.

Approach
  • Restructured the leadership and the team, redeploying people cost-neutrally rather than cutting headcount to hit a number
  • Renegotiated or exited the unprofitable referral contracts driving the losses, one relationship at a time
  • Found and won an anchor instruction — The Victoria, the regeneration of the former Glasgow hospital — purposefully displacing a major competitor to give the team something to rally behind
Result

The unit became one of the division's stronger performers, and the recovery earned promotion to Divisional Director.

05 TBC
Cost → Profit two cost centres turned self-funding

Turning cost centres into profit centres

Situation

Marketing and market research were treated as pure cost centres — necessary spend with no expectation of return — while cross-sell and financial-services income were left largely to chance rather than engineered into how transactions were run.

Approach
  • Brought business services in-house and ran them as commercial functions, not overheads, giving the division direct control of cost and output
  • Took control of the transaction process itself to engineer cross-sales rather than hope for them, lifting attached financial-services income
  • Redesigned remuneration to reward cross-sell and financial-services behaviour directly — the same incentive-realignment principle behind the Win:Sell model
Result

Marketing and market research became self-funding income streams, protecting both from budget cuts, while four of five divisions ranked in the group's top four of thirty-six for cross-sell.

06 Trusted Relationships
c.2,500 units c.£12.5m personal contribution, 35% new business overall

Trusted enough to stay in the room

Situation

The biggest wins didn't come from a single big pitch — they came from staying in relationships long after most people would have moved on, and from being trusted with national accounts run through office networks and managers entirely outside the formal reporting line.

Approach
  • Won a landmark 500-unit Liverpool conversion after seven years of sustained pursuit — one of the largest single instructions in the account's history
  • Held national accounts like Sanctuary, Thriving (PfP), CBRE and Octopus Real Estate through consultative advice first, then delivery — with 100% retention and exclusive rights secured on two
  • Drove standardisation across office networks outside the direct reporting line by setting the model and targets they'd own, not by issuing instructions
Result

c.2,500 units won personally — c.£12.5m of contribution — with new-business wins up 35% year on year overall, built on relationships that outlasted any single deal.

07 Governance
£1.4m year-on-year rise in new-business wins, Q1

An extra 1%, every day

Situation

Reporting was manual, AML compliance checks were slow and inconsistent, and there was no live view of the sales pipeline — improvement depended on whoever happened to notice a problem, not a system built to surface it.

Approach
  • Designed and built the group CRM from nothing, as both a growth tool and a governance platform
  • Automated AML compliance and replaced manual reporting with live management information, feeding a weekly coaching and pipeline cadence
  • Used the same cadence to find touch points where small, daily improvements compounded — an extra 1%, repeated, rather than one big fix
Result

A £1.4m year-on-year rise in new-business wins in the first full quarter, with compliance and reporting now running on systems rather than manual effort — and the same cadence still driving coaching today.
