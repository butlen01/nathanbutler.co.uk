Trusted Relationships

Trusted enough to stay in the room

How patience on a seven-year pursuit, and consultative advice-first account management, built relationships that outlasted any single deal.

c.2,500 units c.£12.5m personal contribution, 35% new business overall

Situation

The biggest wins in this track record didn't come from a single strong pitch. They came from two different kinds of trust: staying in a relationship long after most people would have moved on to easier opportunities, and being handed national accounts run through office networks and managers entirely outside the formal reporting line — with no obligation to prioritise what mattered to those accounts.

Approach

  • Pursued a landmark 500-unit conversion instruction with a major independent developer for seven years — the kind of opportunity that surfaces once, and is easy to lose to a competitor with a longer memory or more patience. Stayed in the room, kept the relationship live and the business front-of-mind long after most would have moved on.
  • Held national accounts like Sanctuary, Thriving (PfP), CBRE and Octopus Real Estate by winning the advisory mandate first — consultative B2B advice on go-to-market strategy, pricing, product mix and phasing — then owning delivery, wholly accountable for the outcome.
  • Drove standardisation across office networks outside the direct reporting line by setting the model and the targets those networks would own, rather than by issuing instructions they had no reason to follow.

Result

The Liverpool conversion was won — one of the largest single instructions in the account's history, proof that some of the biggest wins are a test of patience as much as pitch. Every national account was retained, with exclusive rights secured on two. Across the two, c.2,500 units won personally, c.£12.5m of contribution, with new-business wins up 35% year on year overall.
