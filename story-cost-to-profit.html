Revenue Diversification

Turning Cost Centres Into Profit Centres

Two standing cost centres and a third-party dependency, each turned into an income line, part of the wider margin campaign that held return on sale through a three-year downturn.

Cost → Profit two cost centres turned self-funding

Situation

Marketing and market research were treated as pure cost centres: necessary spend with no expectation of a return, the first lines cut when budgets tightened. Both were valued only as business-winning tools, ways to make the offering stand out, which they did, but neither earned a penny directly. Financial services followed a similar pattern from a different angle: the division relied on third-party providers, mainly because their service offering was better than anything built in-house, so that income sat entirely outside the business.

Approach

A Research Product, Not a Research Cost

I changed the report format market research produced and started selling it, up to £15k a time, developer by developer, then rolled out training to every BDM so the product had a consistent pitch behind it, not just a good idea sitting in a filing cabinet.

The Levy That Turns a Small Number Into Profit

The old logic was that marketing's salary cost, divided across every developer the team served, worked out small per account, barely worth billing for. I inverted that: every piece of marketing material produced on a developer's behalf now carried a modest levy. Divided small is still a cost. Charged per developer, the same spend becomes upside.

Building a Reason for Financial Services to Say Yes

Rather than accept the third-party dependency, I engaged the stakeholders directly and made the case for a bespoke arrangement: a service built specifically around this business, in exchange for a genuine promise of incremental income for them. In return, the business shared in that incremental income itself, turning a service the division had always paid for, in effect, into one that paid back.

The effect showed up directly in the average fee per sale, not just as a separate income line. A mortgage sale rides on a house sale that's already happening, so there's no material extra cost to deliver it, and the average £560 it added to the P&L per transaction dropped almost straight to margin. A higher fee earned at close to zero marginal cost is one of the cleanest routes to a higher return on sale there is, cleaner than any amount of extra volume.

Result

Marketing and market research stopped being cost lines and became income lines, which changed what happened to them at the next budget round: neither was ever seriously threatened with cuts again. Four of five divisions ranked in the group's top four of thirty-six for cross-sell, proof the model worked well beyond the division where it was first tried. Financial services alone added an average of £560 per sale at close to zero marginal cost, which drops almost entirely to margin because the mortgage sale rides on a house sale that is already happening. That is one of the cleanest routes to a higher return on sale there is, and cleaner than any amount of extra volume. It also improved the customer's position rather than exploiting it. A new-build, off-plan buyer needs advice built around exchange and completion timescales that a standard high street process is not shaped for, and bringing that capability in-house meant the advice was designed around the transaction the buyer was actually making.
