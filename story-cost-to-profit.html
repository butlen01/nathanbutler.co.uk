Revenue Diversification

Turning cost centres into profit centres

How in-housing business services and engineering cross-sell into the transaction process turned two overheads into self-funding income streams.

Cost → Profit two cost centres turned self-funding

Situation

Marketing and market research were treated as pure cost centres — necessary spend with no expectation of a return, the first lines cut when budgets tightened. At the same time, cross-sell activity and financial-services income were left largely to chance: something that happened if the right conversation occurred, rather than something engineered into how transactions were actually run.

Approach

  • Brought business services in-house and ran them as commercial functions with their own targets and accountability, not overheads to be minimised.
  • Took control of the transaction process itself to engineer cross-sales systematically rather than hope individual advisors would think to make the introduction.
  • Redesigned remuneration to reward cross-sell and financial-services behaviour directly — applying the same incentive-realignment principle behind the Win:Sell model to a different part of the business.
  • Measured the two former cost centres against the same commercial standards as any other revenue line, rather than as protected departments exempt from budget pressure.

Result

Marketing and market research became self-funding income streams, protecting both from the next round of budget cuts, while four of five divisions ranked in the group's top four of thirty-six for cross-sell — income earned through a redesigned process, not luck.
