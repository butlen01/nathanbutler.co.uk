Turnaround

Rescuing a business earmarked for closure

How a loss-making Scottish unit was restructured, its team rebuilt, and an anchor win used to turn belief around as much as the numbers.

Loss → Profit a unit earmarked for closure

Situation

A Scottish business unit was six figures loss-making and earmarked for closure. Its contract base was built on referral arrangements that were fundamentally unprofitable — the more business they generated, the more the unit lost. Beyond the financial position, the team itself had stopped believing the unit could be turned around, which made the rebuild as much a leadership problem as a commercial one.

Approach

  • Restructured the leadership and the team, redeploying people cost-neutrally rather than cutting headcount purely to hit a number — the priority was rebuilding a team that could actually deliver a recovery, not just shrinking the cost base.
  • Renegotiated or exited the unprofitable referral contracts driving the losses, one relationship at a time, accepting short-term revenue loss to stop the bleeding.
  • Found and won an anchor instruction — The Victoria, the regeneration of the former Victoria Hospital in Glasgow — purposefully pursued to displace a major incumbent competitor at a point where the unit had nothing else to point to.
  • Used the win as the turning point for the team, not just the balance sheet — proof, visible to everyone, that the unit could still win against strong competition.

Result

Completed as Divisional Director: stabilised, then repriced fees from strength as agent of choice, having carried the sales costs to win the account back from weakness. The unit became one of the division's stronger performers, and the recovery earned promotion to Divisional Director.
