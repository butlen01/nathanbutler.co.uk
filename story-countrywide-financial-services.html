Company Case Study — Countrywide Financial Services

Bringing an In-House Function Back In-House

Countrywide Financial Services, the group's own financial services arm, existed the whole time — but the new-homes division routed almost all of its mortgage and protection business to outside providers instead. This is how that referral stream was brought back in-house, and why the harder part was persuading a function that didn't report to me, not the client.

+£560 average added per sale, at close to zero marginal cost

Situation

Countrywide Financial Services, the group's own financial services arm, existed the whole time. It wasn't a capability the business needed to build or a provider it needed to find — it already sat inside the same organisation, under a different part of the matrix. And yet the new-homes division routed almost all of its mortgage and protection business to outside providers instead.

The reason wasn't inertia. Countrywide Financial Services' existing model had grown up around a different kind of buyer and a different kind of transaction, and it had never been reshaped around new-build, off-plan purchases — the specific rhythm of reservation, exchange and completion a new-homes sale runs on. Every referral sent externally instead was income leaving a group that already had the capability to earn it, just not yet in a form the new-homes business could actually use.

Winning Trust Inside the Matrix

This wasn't a vendor negotiation. Countrywide Financial Services had its own leadership, its own priorities, and no reporting line to the new-homes division asking for change — the same structural problem that ran through every account built on influence rather than authority, except this time the account was internal. Nobody could be instructed to build a bespoke service model for a division that had, until now, sent them almost no business.

The case had to be made in commercial terms Countrywide Financial Services' own leadership would recognise: not a favour, but an opportunity — an entire referral stream that had been leaving the group for years, available to bring in-house if the service model changed to fit it. That reframing, from a request to an opportunity, is what actually moved the conversation.

+£560 Average added per sale, at close to zero marginal cost
In-House Income redirected from external providers back into the group
Zero Reporting line between the two functions — this was influence, not instruction

Approach

Understanding Why the Referrals Left

Before anything could change, the actual reason the business had defaulted to external providers had to be understood properly, not assumed. It came down to fit: Countrywide Financial Services' existing model wasn't built around the timeline and buyer profile of a new-homes sale, so the path of least resistance had always been an outside provider whose process already matched.

Building a Service Model That Actually Fit

Worked with Countrywide Financial Services' own stakeholders to build a bespoke arrangement shaped around new-homes buyers specifically, then integrated the offer directly into the sales process itself — raised at the point a buyer was already committing, not chased afterwards as a separate referral.

Structuring the Incentive

The arrangement was structured so the new-homes division accrued a share of the incremental income the redirected business created — an internal revenue share rather than a one-off favour, which is what made the change durable rather than a single win that faded once the goodwill did.

Result

An average of £560 added to the P&L per sale, at close to zero marginal cost, on income that used to leave the group entirely. One of the cleanest routes to a higher return on sale there is, and proof that the biggest commercial wins aren't always external. Sometimes the account that needs winning is the one already inside your own building.
