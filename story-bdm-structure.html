BDM Structure

A BDM structure built to scale, local to national

How three tiers of business development, all aligned to the same incentive model, turned individual relationships into a repeatable pipeline.

£1.1bn GDV pipeline, contracts secured

Situation

The land and business-development function ran on individual relationships and ad hoc coverage. Some offices had strong local networks; others had almost none. There was no consistent model for how an opportunity — a site, a landowner introduction, a developer relationship — actually got pursued, or handed from the person who found it to the team who could deliver it.

That gap showed up as inconsistency in the pipeline: strong quarters in some regions, weak ones in others, with no structural reason either way beyond who happened to be in the room.

Approach

  • Built a three-tier BDM structure — local, regional, national — each aligned to the same Win:Sell incentive model, not competing versions of it, so a national opportunity and a local one were pursued under the same rules.
  • Standardised the land operating model so opportunities were qualified, progressed and handed off the same way regardless of which office found them — removing the dependency on any one person's personal network.
  • Used the aligned structure to build deeper, longer B2B relationships with landowners, agents and developers, rather than one-off pursuits chased opportunistically.
  • Made the tiers genuinely complementary rather than overlapping — local teams built and held relationships, regional teams coordinated across sites, national teams took the biggest and most complex instructions — so growth in one tier reinforced the others instead of competing with them for credit.

Result

A pipeline built on a repeatable structure, not individual heroics — c.£1.1bn of GDV under contract across the portfolio, sourced through relationships the model was built to sustain rather than relationships that happened to exist.
