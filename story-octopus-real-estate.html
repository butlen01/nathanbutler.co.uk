Octopus Real Estate: the Sales and Compliance Operation, Built From Scratch

Octopus Real Estate, part of a group better known for energy and utilities, chose to deploy capital into housing. This is how the sales operation and the regulatory compliance framework behind it, built to Homes England's Capital Funding Guide, were built from a blank page, with no existing function to inherit, on sites the length of the country, and run through to completion.

Full Lifecycle brought in pre-launch, sites from Bath to North Devon

Situation

Octopus Real Estate is the property investment arm of Octopus Energy, a utility provider that chose to deploy capital into housing rather than energy infrastructure alone. The model was straightforward on paper: buy developments from housebuilders, convert them into shared ownership and affordable housing products, and hold the resulting income. In practice it meant becoming, almost overnight, a housing provider bound by rules a utility company had never had to operate under.

The portfolio ran the length of the country, Bath, North London, Northam and Bideford in the South West among a plethora of sites, and I was brought in before any of it was sales-ready. Not to fix a process that already existed, but to build one from a blank page, then run it through to every building's completion.

The Capital Funding Guide

Shared ownership funded through Homes England's Capital Funding Guide comes with rules that determine who is even allowed to buy, not just how the sale is marketed. Household income is capped at £80,000 a year outside London and £90,000 within it, directly relevant on the North London scheme, where the higher threshold changed who the marketing could target in the first place. Applicants are primarily expected to be first-time buyers, though existing owners can qualify in the right circumstances, and serving armed forces personnel and recently discharged veterans receive priority where supply is limited.

None of that is a box-ticking exercise. The affordability assessment has to establish that a buyer can sustain home ownership long-term, not just afford the initial purchase: savings, other assets and outgoings all examined, not only income. Existing homeowners generally have to sell their current property before or alongside completing a shared ownership purchase. Get any of it wrong and the exposure isn't a difficult conversation with a buyer, it's a compliance finding against a government-funded programme.

Approach

Building the Go-to-Market Strategy From Nothing

There was no existing sales function to inherit, so the brief started with a blank page: go-to-market strategy, branding, pricing and specification for every scheme, built before a single unit went on sale. Sales delivery followed, and ran through to completion on every site, not handed off once the launch was done.

Proving Compliance, Not Just Managing It

Understanding what the Capital Funding Guide actually required was only half the job. The other half was building the audit trail that proved it: records showing every buyer had been assessed correctly, every rent-to-unsold-equity percentage held to what was agreed at initial allocation, every scheme ready to withstand scrutiny from Homes England without a scramble to reconstruct the paperwork after the fact. That protection is what let a utility company new to housing operate with the confidence of one that had been doing it for years.

A National Portfolio, Run From the North West

Every site sat hundreds of miles from home, Bath, North London, Northam, Bideford, and none of it was managed any less closely for the distance. Regional oversight, frequent and accurate reporting, and enough personal visibility on each site meant a problem in the South West was never a surprise discovered from the North West.

The Commercial Lens Behind the Compliance

A mandate this regulated could easily have narrowed into pure administration. It didn't, because the compliance discipline sat underneath a genuinely commercial operation: pricing and specification decisions were tested against what the affordability rules would actually allow a buyer to pay, not set first and reconciled with the rules afterward. Go-to-market strategy was built to match what the business could actually deliver on-site, not what looked ambitious on a slide. That alignment between strategic intent and operational capacity, held together with the same stakeholder management and personal accountability any managing director's brief would demand, is the difference between running a sales function and running a business.

Commercial Outcome

Account retained on the same terms as the group's most established housing clients, on a national portfolio built from a blank page and run from the North West.

Customer Outcome

Affordability assessed against whether a buyer could sustain ownership long term rather than merely afford the purchase. Income caps applied correctly, priority honoured for first-time buyers and armed forces personnel, and an audit trail that proved it before Homes England ever asked.
