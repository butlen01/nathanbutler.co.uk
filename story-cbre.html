CBRE Global Investment Management

Abbey Place — the Flagship of CBRE's National Fund Portfolio

CBRE Global Investment Management deploys institutional capital into real estate, buying housing from developers at a discount and converting it into shared ownership and affordable rent. This is how that fund's national housing operation was run — not as a sales mandate, but as something closer to a fractional Chief Operating Officer role — headlined by a 500-unit tower at Abbey Place and a scheme at Bletchley sold out entirely off-plan.

650+ units delivered across eight sites nationally

Situation

CBRE Global Investment Management manages money on behalf of institutional investors, deploying it into real estate. The strategy behind this fund was specific: buy housing units from developers at a discount to open market value, convert them into shared ownership and affordable rent products, then retain the stock as an income-producing asset. Government grant funding, available on those tenures, effectively subsidised part of the conversion cost — which is what let the fund acquire discounted stock and still produce a return its investors would recognise as high-yielding, not merely acceptable.

That model only works if the units are bought right, priced right, and sold at the rate a fund's investors expect — three separate disciplines that don't usually sit with one person. My role wasn't a sales mandate in the way most accounts are. It sat closer to a fractional Chief Operating Officer for the fund's housing operations: involved before a deal was signed, not brought in once the commercial terms were already fixed.

500 units Abbey Place, Abbey Wood — 45% sold off-plan
150 units Bletchley, Milton Keynes — sold out entirely off-plan
650+ units across eight sites nationally

Approach

Sourcing and Advising the Deals

Deals came from two directions: sourced directly and brought to CBRE as opportunities, and evaluated on CBRE's behalf when they found something themselves. Either way, the advice came before a decision to proceed — whether a site's numbers, location and tenure mix actually supported the fund's shared ownership and affordable rent model, not just whether it looked attractive on paper.

Setting the Commercial Case

Once a deal was approved, the work moved to pricing strategy, marketing strategy and product specification — the three levers that determine whether a converted scheme actually performs the way a fund's model assumes it will. Get the specification wrong for the shared ownership buyer it's aimed at, or price ahead of what the affordable rent market will bear, and the yield the fund reports to its investors never materialises.

A National Structure

Delivery ran nationally across eight sites, with regional sales managers reporting directly to me on every scheme — the same structure that let a small number of people run a geographically scattered portfolio without losing sight of any single site's performance.

Abbey Place: the Flagship

The flagship of the portfolio was Abbey Place, a 500-unit tower in Abbey Wood, London. Forty-five per cent of the scheme sold off-plan, with the remainder following within around twelve months of completion — a sell-through rate that justified the fund's underwriting on the scheme before a single unit had even been built.

Bletchley: Sold Before It Was Built

At Bletchley in Milton Keynes, a 150-flat scheme sold out entirely off-plan, every unit committed before completion. It is the cleanest possible proof that the pricing and positioning were right: no incentive scramble at completion, no unsold stock sitting on the fund's balance sheet waiting for a buyer.

Result

Across Abbey Place, Bletchley and roughly six further sites nationally, more than 650 units were delivered for a client whose real product wasn't the housing itself — it was the yield the fund reported to its own investors. Every scheme performed to the model it was underwritten against, which is the only result that actually matters to an institutional fund: not a single good sale, but a portfolio that behaves exactly as promised.
