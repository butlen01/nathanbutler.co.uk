Margin Discipline

Holding Margin While the Market Squeezed It

UK new-build completions fell a cumulative 10.2% over three years, yet return on sale held at 23%. Four levers, pulled at once, protected margin a shrinking market wouldn't hand over on its own.

23% ROS return on sale, sustained

Situation

Over 2023–2026, the UK private housing and new-build sale market faced a severe downturn — a cumulative 10.2% drop in completions since the end of the Help to Buy scheme, with persistent quarterly output contractions reported by the Office for National Statistics throughout.

In a buoyant market, the easiest way to improve return on sale is simply to sell more: once volume passes a certain threshold, incremental income is largely profit, after variable pay and tax. That lever is what most businesses lean on. It barely works when the market itself is contracting — you can't outsell a shrinking pool of buyers your way to a better margin. Protecting return on sale had to come from how the business was run, not from volume the market wasn't going to give up.

Approach

Paying for What Used to Be Free

The business had been giving away site boards and hoarding as a cost of winning instructions — a small, unrecovered expense on every account, and a habit that added up across the whole portfolio. With the new land strategy, I flipped it: developers now paid for these goods themselves. The principle behind this, and every other cost decision through the downturn, was simple — don't cut to hit a number, treat the purse as if it's your own.

An Unfair Share of a Shrinking Market

I pushed productivity per head hard, grinding in the idea that everyone found one per cent more each day. It sounds small. Compounded across a whole team, every day, it wasn't. Winning more mandates than the market's own contraction would suggest meant more opportunities to sell even as the overall pool shrank — taking more than a fair share of a smaller pie, rather than a fair share of a shrinking one.

Fees Worth Holding Out For

I held out on fee negotiations rather than discounting to win work. The best agent's services are worth paying for — but that price has to be earned, not asserted. It came either through consultative advice genuinely demonstrated on pricing and go-to-market strategy, or by bringing the developer a land opportunity directly, the same land-team approach behind the BDM structure elsewhere in this business. Fee discipline without something to justify it is just stubbornness; with it, it's pricing power.

Transferring the Cost, Keeping the People

I restructured commercial terms so staffing costs were billed directly to clients — sometimes at a lower headline fee to get the structure agreed. That trade was deliberate: it moved the cost, and the risk that sat behind it, onto the client relationship, while the business remained the employer and carried the HR burden. A lower fee line that transfers real risk off the balance sheet is a better deal than a higher one that doesn't.

Result

A sustained 23% return on sale, while industry-average returns were being squeezed from 15% down to 10% in the same market — a gap that widened, not narrowed, over the period. None of it came from selling more into a market that wasn't there to sell into.
