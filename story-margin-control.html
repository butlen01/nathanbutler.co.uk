Business Control

Holding margin while the market squeezed it

How taking direct control of the cost base — rather than cutting further — held return on sale steady while industry averages fell.

23% ROS return on sale, sustained

Situation

Return on sale was under constant pressure. Industry-average returns were being squeezed from 15% down to 10% across the same period, and the easy costs — the obvious trims — had already been taken. The next gain had to come from how the business was actually run, not from another round of cost-cutting.

Approach

  • Took direct control of the cost base, closing out loss-making lines entirely rather than trimming around them and leaving the underlying problem in place.
  • Brought group services in-house where it removed duplicated cost and gave the division direct control of its own margin, rather than absorbing a shared-services charge it had no influence over.
  • Built the reporting and pipeline discipline needed to see margin erosion early — a live view of where returns were slipping, rather than finding out after the numbers had already moved.
  • Treated margin protection as an ongoing discipline, not a one-off exercise — the same regular-review cadence used to retest pricing and structure elsewhere in the business.

Result

A sustained 23% return on sale, held while industry-average returns were being squeezed from 15% down to 10% in the same market — a gap that widened, not narrowed, over the period.
