Leadership Approach

The smartest person in the room is the one everybody understands.

Complexity doesn't create value. Clarity, alignment and disciplined execution do. These six examples are how commercial and organisational systems actually get rebuilt — and how that change gets delivered through people, many of them outside my reporting line, who could have said no.

The Commercial Model

“Win the client mandate through consultative B2B advice on go-to-market and pricing, then own the B2C sales execution that delivers it — a model that transfers to any business that wins clients and then delivers for them.”

Six Systems, Six Changes

How the work actually gets rebuilt.

01 Systems
£1.4m year-on-year rise in new-business wins, Q1

Systems & structure

Situation

Performance issues were being treated as execution problems. Reporting was manual, AML compliance checks were slow and inconsistent, there was no live view of the pipeline, and coaching had nothing consistent to work from.

Approach

Diagnosed it as a systems gap, not an effort gap, and built the fix as a platform rather than a patch: the group CRM, designed and built from nothing, feeding live management information into a weekly coaching and pipeline cadence, with compliance automated rather than policed.

Result

Reporting and compliance now run on systems, not manual effort — and the same cadence still drives coaching today. A structure that keeps producing, not a one-off intervention.

02 Rescue
Loss → Profit a unit earmarked for closure

Turning a decline into growth

Situation

A Scottish business unit was six figures loss-making and earmarked for closure, its contract base built on unprofitable referral arrangements it couldn't sustain — and the team had stopped believing it could be turned around.

Approach

Reshaped the contract base away from the unprofitable referrals, restructured the leadership, and redeployed the team cost-neutrally to halt the losses — then rebuilt momentum by winning The Victoria, the regeneration of the former Glasgow hospital, purposefully displacing a major competitor to give the team something to rally behind.

Result

The unit became one of the division's stronger performers, and the recovery earned promotion to Divisional Director — proof that a team can be led back from believing it's already lost.

03 Influence
35% uplift in new-business wins, year on year

Winning without the org chart

Situation

A decline in stock holding was being treated as an execution problem — the team was already being pushed harder — when the real cause was an incentive structure that rewarded the wrong behaviour, owned jointly by teams with no formal reason to cooperate.

Approach

Redesigned a long-standing pay framework around a "Win:Sell" model — one team winning instructions, one team selling them, on jointly owned targets — negotiated cost-neutrally so no one was worse off, and landed through influence across teams outside the direct reporting line, not instruction.

Result

New-business wins rose 35% year on year, in a contracting market. The model still runs unchanged.

04 Growth
4× divisional revenue growth, sustained at 23% ROS

Growth held through a hard market

Situation

Divisional revenue had room to grow, but growth that rides a rising market proves nothing about leadership — the real test was whether it could be built and held through a cyclical, often contracting one.

Approach

Kept the commercial case under constant review rather than coasting on what worked last year — retesting pricing, structure and investment on a regular cadence, and simplifying multi-stakeholder complexity into direction that dispersed teams could actually execute against.

Result

Revenue grew from c.£1.9m to c.£8.2m over seven years — more than fourfold — at a sustained 23% return on sale, while industry-average returns were being squeezed from 15% down to 10%.

05 Persistence
500 units one of the largest single instructions won

Staying in the room

Situation

A landmark 500-unit conversion instruction with a major independent developer was the kind of opportunity that surfaces once — and is easy to lose to a competitor with a longer memory or more patience.

Approach

Stayed in the room. Seven years of sustained pursuit, keeping the relationship live and the business front-of-mind long after most would have moved on to easier wins.

Result

Won — one of the largest single instructions in the account's history, and proof that some of the biggest wins are a test of patience as much as pitch.

06 Trust
100% account retention, every national account

Trusted beyond the reporting line

Situation

National accounts like Sanctuary, Thriving (PfP), CBRE and Octopus Real Estate were mostly run through office networks and managers outside the formal reporting line, with no obligation to prioritise what mattered to these accounts.

Approach

Won each mandate on consultative B2B advice first — go-to-market, pricing, product mix, phasing — then earned the standing to align people who could say no, driving standardisation across office networks by setting the model and the targets they'd own, not by issuing instructions.

Result

Every account retained, with exclusive rights secured on two — plus four of five divisions ranking in the group's top four of thirty-six for cross-sell, income earned through influence rather than authority.
