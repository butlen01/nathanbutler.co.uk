Leadership Approach

The smartest person in the room is the one everybody understands.

Complexity doesn't create value. Clarity, alignment and disciplined execution do. These seven principles are how complex, multi-stakeholder commercial challenges get turned into strategies that dispersed teams — many of them outside my reporting line — can actually align behind.

The Commercial Model

“Win the client mandate through consultative B2B advice on go-to-market and pricing, then own the B2C sales execution that delivers it — a model that transfers to any business that wins clients and then delivers for them.”

Seven Principles

How the work actually gets done.

01

Systems and structure, not symptoms

I diagnose whether a performance issue sits in execution, incentives or operating design, then intervene at the level that will actually move it. Pushing a team harder rarely fixes a problem that was built into the structure around them.

See it in the Win:Sell redesign →
02

Commercially inventive

Pricing and commercial structure are strategic tools, not fixed positions. I invest margin where it unlocks larger downstream opportunities, reprice from strength once a position is earned, and build self-funding income streams where none existed — rather than treating every commercial decision as a single transaction.

See it in the cost-centre turnaround →
03

Delivery through influence, not authority

A large part of what I achieve runs through people and functions I don't control. I win and hold national accounts, and drive standardisation across office networks and managers outside my reporting line, by setting the model and the targets they own — not by issuing instructions.

See it in the Sanctuary account →
04

Clarity that creates buy-in

I turn complex, multi-stakeholder challenges into simple operating models people will actually own: incentive structures that aggregate targets and sharpen accountability, and budgets built from the ground up by the managers who deliver them — then managed through a regular performance and pipeline cadence rather than by intervention.

05

Decisive, and fair with it

I make the hard structural calls — replacing long-standing pay frameworks, exiting unprofitable contracts, addressing underperformance directly — while keeping change cost-neutral and redeploying people rather than discarding them.

See it in the Scotland turnaround →
06

Breadth across the commercial stack

P&L, sales, marketing, operations, CRM and incentive design, led together in one role rather than handed off across a dozen functions. That breadth is what lets me see the whole commercial picture, set direction and standards across all of it, and still give capable people the room to run their part.

07

Growth held through a hard market

Divisional revenue grew more than fourfold, and a business unit facing closure returned to sustained profit — in a cyclical residential market, not a rising one. Growth that survives a hard market is a different kind of proof than growth that rides one.
