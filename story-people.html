People and Capability

Grow What You Can, Buy What You Can't

Nine people reported to me. Five of the six senior roles among them were filled from inside the business, the sixth came from the client side, and the ones who left went on to run sales for developers. This is how that was built, and why it was built rather than recruited.

5 of 6 senior roles filled from within

Situation

Regional leadership in this business is specialised, dispersed and hard to hire at level. The market for somebody who understands a developer client, a 250-office agency network and a P&L at the same time is thin, expensive and slow. Every senior vacancy filled externally costs months of momentum and carries a real risk of getting it wrong in public.

The alternative is to build the ladder so that the next person is already most of the way up it before the vacancy exists.

Approach

Five rungs, not two

Business development manager, senior business development manager, Head of New Homes, Residential Development Director, Senior Residential Development Director. Five rungs in one discipline, which means somebody is always one step below every role that matters and already doing a version of it. A structure with two levels in it forces you to recruit. A structure with five promotes.

Readiness demonstrated, not assumed

People on progression plans were given oversight of live projects with structured feedback rather than shadowing, and the people closest to promotion delivered the training and onboarding themselves. Running an induction is the cleanest test of whether somebody is ready to lead, because you cannot teach a job you have not properly understood.

Mentoring between people, not routed through me

I buddied directors to one another so the conversations that shape a career happened without me in the room. Coaching I did myself, because their performance was my number. Mentoring had to sit outside that, or it is performance management with a nicer name on it.

Grow what you can grow, buy what you cannot

The one senior appointment that did not come up the ladder was the Land Director, who had been my client for ten years. Major land acquisition, the finance underneath it and the judgement about which sites are worth pursuing are not capabilities a sales business develops internally. So I stopped trying to and bought them instead, a deliberate increase in headcount rather than a replacement for one already there.

Result

Five of six senior roles filled from inside, including two Residential Development Directors who started as business development managers, and a Senior Residential Development Director promoted from the tier below. One Head of New Homes left the business and came back a rung higher than the job they had resigned from.

The Land Director was a deliberate increase in headcount, not a backfill: a role added through strategic foresight because major land acquisition, the finance underneath it and the judgement about which sites were worth pursuing needed a dedicated seat at the table to actually execute, capability the ladder could not grow on its own. It changed the unit economics too. Materially larger sites transacted for broadly the same effort, and a bigger site carries more downstream units, more sales revenue and more cross-sold financial services behind it. One extra seat at the table, and a bigger outcome because of it.

And some of the people I developed left. Several went on to Head of Sales and Sales Director roles with developers, and then put business my way. That is the part I would point to first. Promoting my own people proved my judgement to myself. People I trained being hired at senior level by my own clients, and then choosing to work with me again, is the market reaching the same conclusion independently.
