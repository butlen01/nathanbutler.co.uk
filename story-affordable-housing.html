Affordable Housing

Bringing a National Affordable Housing Operation Under Divisional Leadership

A specialist national team, a different client base, a different regulator and a different buyer entirely. This is how it was absorbed into divisional leadership without flattening the specialism that made it work.

760 Homes delivered in the final year

Situation

Affordable housing ran as a separate national team, selling shared ownership and affordable tenures to a buyer profile, on a funding model, and against a regulatory framework that had almost nothing in common with open market new build. Different clients: registered providers, local authorities and institutional funds rather than housebuilders. Different route to market: formal procurement rather than relationship. Different buyer: often first-time, always assessed against eligibility and affordability rules before they are permitted to buy at all. Bringing it under divisional leadership meant absorbing a specialist operation into a general one without flattening the specialism that made it work.

Approach

A small team, under ten people, already delivering but at levels that undersold what a specialist unit that size could produce. The job was growth and integration together: raise output without flattening the different buyer, different regulator and different route to market that made the team distinct in the first place.

Winning Trust With Senior Professionals Who Didn't Report to Anyone in the Business

This wasn't a job I could run from a distance while a team executed underneath me. It was the day job as much as it was direction from above: dealing directly with senior professionals inside registered providers, local authorities and institutional funds, people who answered to their own boards and their own funding rules, not to mine. Earning the standing to be taken seriously in those conversations took real authority, not the authority of a reporting line, since none of them had one to me, but the kind built through understanding their funding conditions, their governance and their own commercial pressures well enough to be trusted with the next instruction.

Grew affordable housing from 10% to 35% of the division's revenue, a deliberate ceiling rather than a natural one: other divisions let the same income stream drift toward 70% of their own number in some instances, and grew comfortable enough on it to win proportionately less new business elsewhere. The team covered roughly half the country and still delivered close to 45% of the group's entire national affordable housing revenue, work worth something like 60–65% of everything every other division delivered across all their revenue streams combined.

Financial Advice a First-Time Buyer Could Actually Trust

The buyer base here was overwhelmingly first-time, which made compliant financial advice close to a necessity rather than a service add-on. Integrating mortgage and protection advice into the sales process, the same financial-services integration running across the wider division (see the Countrywide Financial Services story →), mattered more here than almost anywhere else in the business: getting it wrong wasn't a lost sale, it was a buyer placed in a home they couldn't actually sustain.

A Second Revenue Line Inside the Same Relationships

Strong relationships with registered providers opened a second revenue line beyond new-build sales. When a shared owner wanted to sell, they were required to tell their housing association first, which ran a nominations period: a window in which the HA had first right to find a buyer. HAs instructed my team to market the home during that period, and where it remained unsold once the nominations period closed, we contracted directly with the shared owner to sell it on the open market. The second stream came from stock the HAs and councils needed to move on themselves: end-of-lifecycle properties, derelict, beyond economical repair, or outside current EPC requirements, that didn't fit a standard sales process. I set up contracts through the department to handle that disposal work too.

Result

760 units delivered in the final year, added into the divisional P&L, with a client base of registered providers, local authorities and institutional funds that tenders through formal procurement and buys on delivery evidence rather than relationship. It diversified the division's revenue away from open market cyclicality at exactly the point the open market was contracting, which is worth as much as the volume itself.

A buyer group that is by definition financially stretched, frequently first-time, and assessed against eligibility and affordability rules before they can buy at all. Getting that right is not a service question. It is whether somebody can sustain the home they have just bought, and whether public money produced the outcome it was allocated for.
