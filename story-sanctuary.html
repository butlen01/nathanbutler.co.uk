Sanctuary

Sanctuary — the Largest Account in the Group, Run as One

The largest account in the group by volume, spread across sites the length of the UK from a single Worcester head office. This is how it was run as one national programme, not a collection of local sales instructions — and why every tender since only grew stronger.

c.2,500 units won, the largest account in the group by volume

Situation

Sanctuary was the largest account in the group by volume — more completions running through it than any other client in the business — and it wasn't run from one office. Its own head office sat in Worcester, but its sales management teams were regional: a sales manager and a sales director in every part of the country I needed to work with, none of them reporting to me.

The real stakes weren't sales figures. Sanctuary reported customer satisfaction scores straight from its own buyers to its own board, and my delivery had to hold to that measure, not just to a number I was accountable for internally. Getting that wrong wouldn't just cost a sale — it would show up in a report Sanctuary's board read every quarter.

Approach

Advice First, Then Delivery

Won the advisory mandate before a single sale was made: input on site acquisition, pricing, specification and go-to-market strategy, the same consultative model that ran across every national account. Delivery followed the advice rather than sitting apart from it — take the site, resource it with our own sales team, then wrap a sales management function around it.

A Layer of My Own

Running that at national scale meant building a structure underneath it: regional sales managers recruited from within the business, each holding a number of sites and reporting directly to me. Add the local agency branch network, engaged for incremental footfall and held to a deliberately stringent set of KPIs, and the account effectively had its own dedicated Sales Director — me, fractionally, with a team built to match.

Close to the Detail, Clear at the Top

None of that worked without discipline underneath it. Procedures were recorded and roles were clear enough that anyone could step onto a site and know exactly what they were accountable for — an approach closer to formal project management than a typical sales operation. Reporting was frequent and accurate enough that I could read viewing numbers, staff attendance and sale trajectory on any site without needing to ask: close enough to the detail to catch a problem early, high enough above it to run the whole account, not just one site.

Nothing Was Ever a Surprise

I visited every site personally. Understanding the micro and macro economics of a location before it became a live sales instruction meant risk could be assessed ahead of a problem, not discovered after one — the difference between managing a crisis and never having one.

Result

Every tender cycle made the position stronger. The depth of understanding built up over years, not weeks, meant competitors couldn't match it, and the account was retained in full throughout — still the largest in the group by volume. It is the clearest proof of a wider pattern: the accounts that last aren't won once, they're re-earned every cycle by knowing more than anyone else is willing to learn.
