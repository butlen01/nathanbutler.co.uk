Sanctuary

Sanctuary — the Largest Account in the Group, Run as One

The largest account in the group by volume, spread across sites the length of the UK from a single Worcester head office. This is how it was run as one national programme, not a collection of local sales instructions — and why every tender since only grew stronger.

c.2,500 units won, the largest account in the group by volume

Situation

Sanctuary was the largest account in the group by volume — more completions running through it than any other client in the business — and it wasn't run from one office. Its own head office sat in Worcester, but its sales management teams were regional: a sales manager and a sales director in every part of the country I needed to work with, none of them reporting to me, running sites that were live and selling simultaneously in different parts of the UK.

The real stakes weren't sales figures. Sanctuary measured customer satisfaction directly from its own buyers — the people who had actually bought a home — and reported those scores to its own board. My delivery had to hold to that measure, not just to a sales number I was accountable for internally. A weak month on one site in one region wouldn't just cost a sale; it would show up in a report Sanctuary's board read every quarter, against a client whose name was on the door.

The Leadership Challenge

None of the people whose cooperation I actually needed reported to me. Every regional sales manager and sales director inside Sanctuary had their own priorities, their own targets, and no obligation to make mine easier. Instruction wasn't an option — I could not tell a sales director three hundred miles away how to run their team. The only lever available was making our processes so obviously aligned with theirs, and our people so visibly good at the job, that working closely with us became the easier choice, not the compliant one.

That meant learning Sanctuary's own reporting standards well enough to anticipate them, not just respond to them. If their board wanted to see customer satisfaction trending a certain way, I needed to know that before a regional director had to raise it with me — training my own site teams to the standard Sanctuary's board expected, not the minimum a contract required.

Approach

Advice First, Then Delivery

Won the advisory mandate before a single sale was made: consultative input on site acquisition, pricing, specification and go-to-market strategy on new sites, shaping what got built as well as how it got sold. The operating model that followed was consistent everywhere: take the site from Sanctuary, resource it with our own salespeople, then wrap a sales management function around that team so delivery was never left to whoever happened to be on site that day.

A Layer of My Own

Running that model across dozens of live sites nationally meant it could not be held together personally. I selected people from within our own organisation to act as regional sales managers, each one accountable for a number of Sanctuary sites and reporting directly to me — a structure that meant Sanctuary effectively had a dedicated Sales Director, fractionally, backed by a proper team rather than one person spread too thin. On top of that, local estate agency branch networks were engaged to drive incremental footfall to site, working to a deliberately stringent set of KPIs so performance could be monitored, measured and improved rather than left to hope.

Governance Behind the Scenes

Setting a project like this up needed an approach closer to formal project management than a typical sales operation — not software, but the same discipline: outputs recorded, procedures documented, every person in the structure trained into their role and clear on what they were responsible for. Sales reporting had to be frequent and accurate, not because the paperwork mattered for its own sake, but because it was the only way to run a national account strategically rather than reactively, region by region, without waiting for a problem to surface on its own.

Close to the Detail, Clear at the Top

That governance gave me the ability to be close to the detail and operate at a high level at the same time — viewing numbers, staff attendance, sale trajectory on any individual site, without needing to ask. Most of the value in that closeness wasn't catching problems. It was recognising, early, that something was drifting before it became one.

Nothing Was Ever a Surprise

I visited every site Sanctuary was building on personally. Understanding the micro-economics of a location — footfall patterns, local competition, the specific buyer profile a scheme would attract — and the macroeconomics around it meant a problem could be assessed and planned for before it occurred, not discovered afterwards. It made the whole relationship a better journey for Sanctuary, not just a better-managed one.

Commercial Outcome

Largest account in the group by volume, retained in full through every tender cycle, with the position strengthening each round rather than being defended each round. c.2,500 units won across the relationship.

Customer Outcome

Satisfaction scored by Sanctuary's own buyers and reported to their own board. I was held to that measure rather than to a sales number, and trained my site teams to the standard their board expected rather than the minimum the contract required.
