Client Case Studies

What it delivered for the accounts themselves.

Winning a mandate is the easy part to talk about. This is what happened next, for the funds, housing associations and developers who actually depended on the work being delivered. Anyone can point to a win. The test of a commercial leader is what the client had afterwards, and whether they stayed.

c.2,500 units, national accounts 100% account retention £1.1bn GDV overseen 250 office delivery network
Sanctuary
01 National Account
c.2,500 units won, every one retained

Sanctuary — a blue-chip account, every unit retained

Context

Sanctuary's needs ran across sites the length of the country, not one location, which meant the account needed genuine project management, not just a series of local sales instructions loosely held together. It sat among a set of blue-chip funds and housing associations I won and led as major national accounts, most from outside my formal reporting line, on influence rather than authority.

Approach

Won the advisory mandate first, with consultative B2B advice on go-to-market strategy, pricing, product mix, phasing and sales method. Then owned the delivery, project-managing it as a single UK-wide programme rather than site by site, wholly accountable for the outcome and not just the recommendation.

Result

Every account retained, with exclusive rights secured on two of them. In a market where accounts move on price, none of these did.

The Victoria, Glasgow
02 Landmark Win
Pitch win of the year across the whole group

The Victoria, Glasgow

Context

The regeneration of the former Victoria Hospital in Glasgow, a landmark instruction pursued deliberately as the anchor for a loss-making Scottish unit's recovery. The kind of win that changes how a whole business is perceived, not only its numbers.

Approach

Won purposefully against a major incumbent competitor, at a point where the unit had nothing else to point to. The win became the group's pitch win of the year.

Result

The launch pad for the growth that followed, and the anchor instruction that both funded the turnaround and proved it could be done.

The Tobacco Warehouse, Liverpool
03 7-Year Pursuit
500 units one of the largest single instructions won

The Tobacco Warehouse — 500 units, won after seven years

Context

The Tobacco Warehouse conversion in Liverpool, a landmark instruction with a major independent developer and one of the largest single instructions in the account's history. The kind of opportunity that surfaces once and is easily lost to a competitor with a longer memory or more patience.

Approach

Stayed in the room. Seven years of sustained business development, keeping the relationship live and front of mind long after most people would have moved on to something that paid off faster.

Result

Won, and delivered: 500 units, and proof that patience applied deliberately is a commercial strategy, not just good manners.

Abbey Place — CBRE Global Investment Management
04 Institutional Fund
Full Mandate sales, marketing, compliance and funding, one team

Abbey Place — running the full CBRE fund mandate

Context

Abbey Place, held by CBRE Global Investment Management as an institutional fund, meant sales and marketing execution had to sit inside strict compliance and capital funding guidelines, not run alongside them as a separate concern.

Approach

Took on the full mandate on the fund's behalf: sales, marketing, and the compliance and capital funding requirements that came with institutional money, built into the delivery rather than checked after the fact.

Result

A fund client able to hand over sales and marketing in full, confident that compliance and capital funding requirements were being met without needing to check.

Barnes Village, Cheadle
05 Heritage Conversion
£1.35m 156 homes, Grade II listed

Barnes Village, Cheadle — a Grade II listed hospital site turned 156 homes

Context

The former Barnes Hospital in Cheadle, Grade II listed and disused, needed a route to market that respected its heritage status while making 156 homes genuinely sellable.

Approach

Built the go-to-market strategy around the site's heritage rather than around it, positioning the listed building's history as the sale rather than a constraint the sale had to work past.

Result

£1.35m of business secured and 156 homes sold on a site most agencies would have treated as a liability, not an asset.

WV Living
06 Local Authority
Proof of Concept a council-backed brand selling on commercial terms

WV Living — proving a council-backed brand could sell

Context

Wolverhampton City Council needed WV Living, its housing brand, to prove it could compete and sell credibly in the open market, not only deliver socially.

Approach

Won and led the instruction representing WV Living in the market, applying the same commercial rigour used on private and institutional accounts to a public-sector-backed brand.

Result

Demonstrated the model works: a council-backed developer selling on genuinely commercial terms, opening the door to further local-authority mandates.

Octopus Real Estate
07 New Entrant
New to Housing a utility provider's first move into the sector

Octopus Real Estate — a utility provider's first step into housing

Context

Octopus Real Estate, part of a group better known for energy and utilities, was entering housing for the first time and needed a partner who could bring genuine sales and marketing credibility to a sector they hadn't sold in before.

Approach

Won the mandate on the same consultative model used across every national account, advice first, then delivery, giving a housing-market outsider a proven route in rather than a standing start.

Result

A national account retained on the same terms as the group's most established housing clients, despite Octopus Real Estate being entirely new to the sector.
