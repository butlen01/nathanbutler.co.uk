Client Case Studies

What it delivered for the accounts themselves.

Winning a mandate is the easy part to talk about. This is what happened next: for the funds, housing associations, councils and developers who depended on the work actually being delivered, and for the people who bought and moved into the homes at the end of it. Anyone can point to a win. The test of a commercial leader is what the client had afterwards, whether the customer was better off, and whether the account was still there at the next tender. A large share of it was regeneration rather than greenfield: a former infirmary, a Victorian hospital, a listed dock warehouse and council-owned land brought forward for homes that were genuinely needed.

c.4,500 units, national accounts 100% account retention, zero accounts lost £1.1bn GDV overseen 250 office delivery network
Public & Social Housing

A housing association and a council-led company, each needing a commercial operation that still answered to their own board or their own electorate.

Sanctuary
01 Largest Account by Volume
c.1,500 the largest account in the group by volume

Sanctuary: the largest account in the group, run as one national programme

Context

Sanctuary ran more completions through it than any other client in the business, spread the length of the UK. Its sales management sat outside my reporting line entirely, regional teams I had to align without authority over them, and its satisfaction scores went straight from buyers to its own board: the standard I was held to wasn't mine to set.

Approach

Won the advisory mandate first, on site acquisition, pricing and go-to-market, then owned delivery outright: my own sales team, my own layer of regional sales managers reporting to me, effectively running as Sanctuary's fractional Sales Director. The local agency network sat on top of that, held to KPIs stringent enough to actually move performance.

Commercial Outcome

Largest account in the group by volume, retained in full through every tender cycle, the position strengthening each round rather than merely defended. c.1,500 units won across the relationship.

Customer Outcome

Satisfaction scored by Sanctuary's own buyers and reported to their own board, so I trained every site team to the standard their board expected, not the minimum the contract required.
WV Living
02 Local Authority
6 Sites Hampton Park to Daisy Bank

WV Living: translating council bureaucracy into a buyer's journey

Context

Wolverhampton City Council set up WV Living to deliver genuinely needed homes on sites like Hampton Park and Daisy Bank. The ambition was never in question; whether a council-led company could sell what it built like a commercial developer, across shared ownership, bulk sale and the open market, very much was.

Approach

Sat in the room where council decisions were made and translated them, in both directions, into something a buyer could actually use: designing the buyer journey, the touchpoints from reservation to completion, and the reporting that let the council see the model working in commercial terms it recognised.

Commercial Outcome

Six sites sold across three routes to market: shared ownership, bulk sale to private rented sector investors and the open market. Bulk sale gave the council committed volume before a single owner-occupier walked a plot, de-risking the wider programme.

Customer Outcome

Every buyer, whether a first-time shared owner or a family buying outright, moved through the same operation without ever feeling they were dealing with a council. Planning conditions, Section 106 obligations and grant rules translated into plain answers to ordinary questions.
Institutional Capital

Funds and investment vehicles that needed the operation sourced, priced and run, not just a sales mandate won.

Abbey Place: CBRE Global Investment Management
03 Fractional COO
650+ units across eight sites nationally

Abbey Place: the flagship of CBRE's national fund portfolio

Context

CBRE Global Investment Management deploys institutional capital into real estate, buying housing units at a discount, converting them to shared ownership and affordable rent, and holding them as high-yielding, grant-topped assets. My role wasn't a sales mandate: closer to a fractional Chief Operating Officer, sourcing and advising on deals before a single unit was priced.

Approach

Sourced deals directly, then owned pricing, marketing and product specification once a deal was approved, shaping the commercial case before it reached a buyer. Delivery ran across eight sites nationally, regional managers reporting to me on every scheme, headlined by a 500-unit tower at Abbey Place and a 150-flat scheme at Bletchley sold out entirely off-plan.

Commercial Outcome

650-plus units across eight sites, every scheme performing against the model it was underwritten on: the only result that matters to an institutional fund is a portfolio that behaves exactly as promised.

Customer Outcome

The end product was shared ownership and affordable rent, priced against what buyers could actually sustain rather than what the fund's model hoped they could: why Bletchley sold out before a flat was finished.
Octopus Real Estate
04 New Entrant
Full Lifecycle brought in pre-launch, sites nationwide

Octopus Real Estate: the sales and compliance operation, built from scratch

Context

Octopus Real Estate is the property investment arm of Octopus Energy, buying developments from housebuilders and converting them into shared ownership products. Sites run the length of the country, from Bath to Northam and Bideford, and I was brought in before any of it was sales-ready, not once the model was already proven.

Approach

Set up the sales process, go-to-market strategy and branding from the ground up, then managed delivery through to completion. Because Octopus funded conversions through Homes England's Capital Funding Guide, the mandate went beyond sales: understanding exactly what the guide required, and building the audit trail that proved it.

Commercial Outcome

Account retained on the same terms as the group's most established housing clients, on a national portfolio built from a blank page and run from the North West.

Customer Outcome

Affordability assessed against whether a buyer could sustain ownership long term, not merely afford the purchase. Income caps applied correctly, priority honoured for first-time buyers and armed forces personnel, before Homes England ever asked.
Landmark Wins

The pitches and regenerations that became the account's calling card.

The Victoria, Glasgow
05 Landmark Win
413 Homes full masterplan for the former Victoria Infirmary

The Victoria, Glasgow: winning the anchor that turned a region around

Context

A failing Scottish unit needed more than a leaner cost base to recover: it needed proof it deserved a client's most significant site. That proof became the former Victoria Infirmary, a 413-home Glasgow redevelopment won against Savills and pursued deliberately as the anchor for the turnaround.

Approach

Sanctuary tested the rebuilt operation on two smaller sites first, both sold out completely, before instructing the Victoria itself. The pitch that won it was built to be remembered: hard-copy presentation books, the area's own history, and a delivery rehearsed until it was as tight as the material.

Commercial Outcome

The group's pitch win of the year, taken from Savills. Phase one ran at breakeven by design, a market-position bet renegotiated once it was proven, anchoring the recovery of a unit earmarked for closure.

Customer Outcome

135 affordable homes in phase one of 413, keeping the former hospital's Nightingale wards on a site the community had a century of attachment to. The pitch led with what it meant for Langside, not the number.
The Tobacco Warehouse, Liverpool
06 7-Year Pursuit
500 units one of the largest single instructions won

The Tobacco Warehouse: winning the world's largest brick building after seven years

Context

Liverpool's Stanley Dock Tobacco Warehouse, the world's largest brick-built building, was the single largest instruction the business had ever had the chance to win: 500-plus apartments beside Everton's new stadium at Bramley-Moore Dock.

Approach

Seven years staying in the room through planning, redesign and reconfiguration, handing over strategic input for free with no signed instruction, while building the trust of the Dublin-headquartered developer's own principals across repeated trips to Ireland.

Commercial Outcome

The single largest mandate the business has ever landed. 500-plus apartments, plus attachment sales income and the publicity value of being named on the city's most recognisable address.

Customer Outcome

Two entirely different buyer journeys run side by side without either undermining the other: overseas investors buying a landmark, and domestic buyers relying on government schemes to make an off-plan heritage purchase achievable.
Barnes Village, Cheadle
07 Heritage Regeneration
£1.35m 156 homes, Grade II listed

Barnes Village, Cheadle: building a premium brand a London developer couldn't run alone

Context

Henley Homes, a London developer, had bought Barnes Hospital, a Grade II listed Victorian landmark in Cheadle, to convert into 156 homes. What the London-based team couldn't deliver at arm's length was the immaculate, design-led local intensity the premium brand demanded.

Approach

Brought in as fractional sales director to own specification with the interior design team and run plot strategy on the ground, holding the listed building's own apartments back from early releases and sequencing every phase to price up, not down, off the last sold comparable.

Commercial Outcome

£1.35m of fee value on 156 homes, at price points South Manchester had not seen on a comparable heritage conversion, achieved through release sequencing rather than a single pricing decision.

Customer Outcome

A finished product held to a genuinely immaculate standard, from show-apartment fit-out through to the last townhouse, because the building's history was the product being sold and buyers were paying precisely for that.
