What it delivered for the accounts themselves.

Winning a mandate is the easy part to talk about. This is what happened next, for the funds, housing associations and developers who actually depended on the work being delivered. Anyone can point to a win. The test of a commercial leader is what the client had afterwards, and whether they stayed.

c.2,500 units, national accounts 100% account retention £1.1bn GDV overseen 250 office delivery network
Sanctuary
01 National Account
c.2,500 units won, every one retained

Sanctuary — a blue-chip account, every unit retained

Context

Sanctuary sat among a set of blue-chip funds and housing associations I won and led as major national accounts, alongside Thriving (Places for People), CBRE and Octopus Real Estate. Most ran from outside my formal reporting line, on influence rather than authority.

Approach

Won the advisory mandate first, with consultative B2B advice on go-to-market strategy, pricing, product mix, phasing and sales method. Then owned the delivery, the sales programme that executed that advice, wholly accountable for the outcome and not just the recommendation.

Result

Every account retained, with exclusive rights secured on two of them. In a market where accounts move on price, none of these did.

The Victoria, Glasgow
02 Landmark Win
Pitch win of the year across the whole group

The Victoria, Glasgow

Context

The regeneration of the former Victoria Hospital in Glasgow, a landmark instruction pursued deliberately as the anchor for a loss-making Scottish unit's recovery. The kind of win that changes how a whole business is perceived, not only its numbers.

Approach

Won purposefully against a major incumbent competitor, at a point where the unit had nothing else to point to. The win became the group's pitch win of the year.

Result

The launch pad for the growth that followed, and the anchor instruction that both funded the turnaround and proved it could be done.

Liverpool Conversion
03 7-Year Pursuit
500 units one of the largest single instructions won

A 500-unit Liverpool conversion, won after seven years

Context

A landmark conversion instruction with a major independent developer, one of the largest single instructions in the account's history. The kind of opportunity that surfaces once and is easily lost to a competitor with a longer memory or more patience.

Approach

Stayed in the room. Seven years of sustained pursuit, keeping the relationship live and front of mind long after most people would have moved on to something that paid off faster.

Result

Won, and delivered: 500 units, and proof that patience applied deliberately is a commercial strategy, not just good manners.
