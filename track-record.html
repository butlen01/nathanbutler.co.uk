Six problems. What changed because of it.

The headline numbers behind six of the hardest problems solved — the system and the thinking behind each one, the how rather than just the result, is on Leadership.

×4 divisional revenue £1.1bn GDV overseen c.2,500 units, national accounts 250 office delivery network
Results & Impact

The headline numbers, case by case.

01 Systems
£1.4m year-on-year rise in new-business wins, Q1

Building the CRM as a growth and governance platform

Problem

Reporting was manual, AML compliance checks were slow and inconsistent, and there was no live view of the sales pipeline to manage performance by.

Result

A £1.4m year-on-year rise in new-business wins in the first full quarter, with compliance and reporting now running on systems rather than manual effort.

The system and the thinking behind it →
02 Rescue
Loss → Profit a unit earmarked for closure

Rescuing a business earmarked for closure

Problem

A Scottish business unit was six figures loss-making and earmarked for closure, its contract base built on unprofitable referral arrangements it couldn't sustain.

Result

Completed as Divisional Director: stabilised, then repriced fees from strength as agent of choice. The unit became one of the division's stronger performers, and the recovery earned promotion to Divisional Director.

The system and the thinking behind it →
03 Turnaround
35% uplift in new-business wins, year on year

Turning a stock decline into 35% growth — cost-neutrally

Problem

A decline in stock holding was being treated as an execution problem. It wasn't — it was an incentive problem, and no amount of pushing the team harder was going to fix that.

Result

New-business wins rose 35% year on year. Sales recovered from a year-on-year decline to growth — in a contracting market, not a rising one.

The system and the thinking behind it →
04 Growth
4× divisional revenue growth over seven years

Growth held through a hard market

Problem

Divisional revenue had room to grow, but a cyclical residential market meant growth that didn't hold through a downturn would prove nothing.

Result

Revenue grew from c.£1.9m to c.£8.2m over seven years — at a sustained 23% return on sale, while industry-average returns were being squeezed from 15% down to 10%.

The system and the thinking behind it →
05 7-Year Pursuit
500 units one of the largest single instructions won

A 500-unit Liverpool conversion, won after seven years

Problem

A landmark conversion instruction with a major independent developer — the kind of opportunity that surfaces once and is easy to lose to a competitor with a longer memory.

Result

Won — one of the largest single instructions in the account's history, after seven years of sustained pursuit.

The system and the thinking behind it →
06 National Accounts
100% account retention, every national account

Trusted with accounts outside the reporting line

Problem

National accounts were mostly run through office networks and managers outside the formal reporting line, with no obligation to prioritise what mattered to these accounts.

Result

Every account retained, with exclusive rights secured on two. Four of five divisions ranked in the group's top four of thirty-six for cross-sell.

The system and the thinking behind it →

Cost centres turned revenue lines. Two standing cost centres — marketing and market research — were reworked into self-funding income streams, protecting both from budget cuts.
